Research line · Structural models

Why structural
models matter.

I use structural models to connect observed choices to preferences, information, and incentives. This helps me study why market outcomes change and what might happen under a different policy or market environment.

Reduced-form methods such as difference-in-differences can identify the effect of an observed change under assumptions such as parallel trends. Structural models add an explicit account of how people and firms make decisions. Their value is the ability to use those mechanisms to address questions beyond the particular change observed in the data.

  • Mechanisms. Separate the roles of preferences, beliefs, information, and constraints in observed behavior.
  • Counterfactuals. Study changes in prices, information, or market rules that have not yet been observed. Models with a supply side can also account for firms’ responses and a new market equilibrium.
  • Welfare. Translate changes in choices into model-based measures of consumer surplus, profits, and distributional effects.

These conclusions depend on identification and on whether the model’s assumptions remain credible in the counterfactual environment. I see reduced-form evidence as a way to discipline and assess a structural model. My two papers below apply this perspective to misrecorded demand data and consumer learning from reviews.

Discrete Choice Models with a Misrecorded Variable

With an Application to Retail Scanner Data

Econometric Methods Advising: Xiaoxia Shi
Industrial Organization Advising: Panle Jia Barwick

How can we learn about demand when a binary variable, such as a promotion indicator, is recorded incorrectly? This paper studies what the data and credible restrictions can identify without requiring a perfectly observed regressor.

Research overview

The approach combines linear programming, moment inequalities, and a monotone instrumental variable to obtain partial-identification bounds. It extends to several discrete choice specifications, including the random-coefficient logit model.

Monte Carlo experiments examine the method. An application to Dominick’s scanner data studies identified ranges for demand parameters and price elasticities, alongside counterfactual simulations.

Follow-up Online Reviews from an Automobile Digital Platform

With Sha Yang · USC Marshall

What do buyers learn from an owner’s initial review and the follow-up reviews posted after months of use? This project studies how the timing and content of owner experiences shape the information available before purchase.

Bayesian learningDiscrete choiceOnline reviewsText measurement

Research overview

The project uses first reviews and follow-up reviews from Autohome. Empirical work examines changes in evaluations within owners, review views and helpful votes, and the relationship between available information, prices paid, and purchases.

A structural model combines Bayesian updating on review signals with logit demand. The model is intended to support counterfactual analysis of an environment without follow-up reviews.

Language-model-based text measurement is another part of this same project, described on the AI in Econ and Marketing page.
