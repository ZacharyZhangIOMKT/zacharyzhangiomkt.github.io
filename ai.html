Personalized Credit Pricing with Fairness across Races and Genders

Offline Policy Learning from Randomized Experiments

Advised by Prof. Kohei Yata

How should a lender personalize credit offers when pricing rules face group-fairness constraints? I use a randomized consumer-credit experiment to learn and evaluate pricing policies, examining outcomes for both the lender and consumers.

Presented at INFORMS Workshop on Data Science 2026 and MVEA 2026

Writing Sample 2 (PDF) · November 2026Presentation details
Offline policy learningCausal inferenceFairness constraintsHeld-out evaluation

Research overview

The paper distinguishes restrictions on the information a pricing rule can use from constraints on average offered rates across groups. It examines the trade-offs between these constraints and lender returns, alongside changes in offers and interest payments.

An ongoing extension considers a lender that learns adaptively through a multi-armed bandit, asking how group-fairness constraints affect learning and consumer outcomes.

Multi-Armed Bandits and Offline Evaluation in Credit Marketing

ECON 726 project · 2025

This project explores what a fixed experimental log can tell us about an adaptive pricing algorithm, using a six-arm Bernoulli bandit environment calibrated to a South African consumer-credit experiment.

Multi-armed banditsUCBOffline replayRegret

Research overview

I compare simulated online learning with offline replay, benchmark regret, and examine the effects of batch updates on take-up. A central lesson is that an apparently favorable regret measure can reflect exhaustion of a fixed log, rather than strong online performance.

This project was a precursor to the credit-offer policy-learning paper.

The Impact of Data Centers on Consumer Behavior

Working with Prof. Kyeongbin (KB) Kim · Wisconsin School of Business

I build news-text datasets and apply NLP to study media coverage of data centers. This developing research direction asks how coverage shapes consumer perceptions and purchasing, within a broader project on how data centers affect local consumers.

Natural language processingText miningMedia coverageConsumer behavior

NLP and media data

I built a Nexis Uni corpus of 18,088 newspaper articles, parsed batch downloads into article-level records, and kept wire stories and press releases separate. I process the article text with NLP and compare newspaper coverage with a national television-coverage series from GDELT.

I am also collecting transcripts from local television stations’ YouTube channels for NLP text mining. The causal strategy for studying the effects of media coverage on consumer perceptions and purchases is still being developed.

Related retail-price analysis

I also built retail-price panels from NielsenIQ scanner data and used matched-county, stacked difference-in-differences to examine whether data-center activations affect frozen-food prices relative to shelf-stable products.

Measuring Consumer Experiences in Review Text

Part of the automobile-review working paper with Prof. Sha Yang

Initial and follow-up reviews need a common measurement scale before their information can be compared. The project uses a fixed-prompt language model to score both types of review and checks these measures against owners’ star ratings.

Natural language processingLLM measurementStar-rating checks

Research overview

The current text-measurement approach uses Qwen2.5-7B. The goal is to turn unstructured accounts of ownership into comparable measures of consumer experience, and connect them to the project’s model of Bayesian learning and discrete choice.

This is a measurement component of the automobile-review research, rather than a separate paper.

Is the Verified Purchase Badge Helpful?

Evidence from Learning in Amazon’s Online Marketplace

ECON 695 course project

I use Sentence-BERT, a Transformer-based sentence-embedding approach, to turn Amazon review text into measures of quality direction, informativeness, and novelty relative to earlier reviews. The project asks whether Verified Purchase badges remain associated with review helpfulness after accounting for the information in review text.

Sentence-BERTTransformer embeddingsBayesian learningDouble machine learning

From Transformer embeddings to economic measures

The analysis uses Musical Instruments reviews posted through 2014 in the Amazon Reviews 2023 dataset. Cosine similarities with prototype sentences and earlier reviews of the same product generate the text measures. I combine these measures with a Gaussian Bayesian learning model and five-fold cross-fitted double machine learning, using neural networks to estimate the nuisance functions.

Because Verified Purchase badges are not randomly assigned, the results describe an adjusted association; a causal interpretation requires additional identifying assumptions.

Precursor to my work with Prof. Sha Yang

This course project was a precursor to my automobile-review project with Prof. Sha Yang. It began my work on using NLP to measure information in reviews and connecting those measures to consumer learning and economic models.
