Personalized Credit Offers Under Fair-Lending Constraints

Offline Policy Learning from a Randomized Consumer-Credit Experiment

Advised by Kohei Yata

How should a lender personalize credit offers when pricing rules face group-fairness constraints? I use a randomized consumer-credit experiment to learn and evaluate pricing policies, examining outcomes for both the lender and consumers.

Presented at INFORMS Workshop on Data Science 2026 and MVEA 2026

Preliminary draft (PDF) · October 2026Presentation details
Offline policy learningCausal inferenceFairness constraintsHeld-out evaluation

Research overview

The paper distinguishes restrictions on the information a pricing rule can use from constraints on average offered rates across groups. It examines the trade-offs between these constraints and lender returns, alongside changes in offers and interest payments.

An ongoing extension considers a lender that learns adaptively through a multi-armed bandit, asking how group-fairness constraints affect learning and consumer outcomes.

Multi-Armed Bandits and Offline Evaluation in Credit Marketing

ECON 726 project · 2025

This project explores what a fixed experimental log can tell us about an adaptive pricing algorithm, using a six-arm Bernoulli bandit environment calibrated to a South African consumer-credit experiment.

Multi-armed banditsUCBOffline replayRegret

Research overview

I compare simulated online learning with offline replay, benchmark regret, and examine the effects of batch updates on take-up. A central lesson is that an apparently favorable regret measure can reflect exhaustion of a fixed log, rather than strong online performance.

This project was a precursor to the credit-offer policy-learning paper.

Measuring Consumer Experiences in Review Text

Part of the automobile-review working paper with Sha Yang

Initial and follow-up reviews need a common measurement scale before their information can be compared. The project uses a fixed-prompt language model to score both types of review and checks these measures against owners’ star ratings.

Natural language processingLLM measurementStar-rating checks

Research overview

The current text-measurement approach uses Qwen2.5-7B. The goal is to turn unstructured accounts of ownership into measures that can be evaluated and connected to an economic model.

This is a measurement component of the automobile-review research, rather than a separate paper.
