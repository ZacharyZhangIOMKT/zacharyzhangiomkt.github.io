I am a senior at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, pursuing a B.S. in Economics, Mathematics, and Data Science, with expected graduation in May 2027.

My research lies at the intersection of industrial organization and quantitative marketing. I combine structural econometrics and causal inference with machine learning and natural language processing to study consumer behavior and marketing decisions.

I study how imperfect information shapes what we can learn about markets and how firms should make decisions. My demand paper uses moment inequalities and linear programming to bound demand parameters when a promotion variable is misrecorded. My credit-pricing paper learns pricing rules from randomized data and examines how fairness constraints affect lender returns and how interest savings are distributed across borrowers.

My research on online reviews uses language models to measure owners’ experiences and connects those measures to a model of consumer learning and purchase decisions. This work grew out of an earlier project using Sentence-BERT embeddings and double machine learning to study review helpfulness. In a related media project, I build news-text datasets and collect local television transcripts to study data-center coverage; the design linking coverage to consumer perceptions and purchasing is still being developed.

This website presents my current research and ideas for combining economic models with AI in measurement, estimation, computation, and counterfactual analysis.

Structural Models

Writing Sample 1. My demand paper studies a misrecorded binary variable using discrete-choice models, linear programming, and moment inequalities. It develops partial-identification bounds and applies them to retail scanner data. Prof. Xiaoxia Shi provided guidance on econometric methods, while Prof. Panle Jia Barwick provided guidance on the industrial organization aspects of the project.

My project with Prof. Sha Yang studies follow-up automobile reviews, how owners update their evaluations after use, and what buyers can learn from those reviews.

Writing Sample 2. My senior thesis with Prof. Kohei Yata studies personalized credit offers under fairness constraints. An earlier ECON 726 project uses multi-armed bandits to study sequential decisions and offline evaluation.
