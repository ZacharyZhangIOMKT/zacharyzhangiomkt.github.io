Research ideas · Future directions

Structural Models × AI

I am interested in combining economic models with machine learning to study how consumers learn, how firms make decisions, and how imperfect information affects empirical conclusions.

These are possible extensions of my current research, not completed studies or established findings.

Measurement & identification

Proposed direction

Text as an imperfect measure

Can information extracted from reviews improve demand estimation when the extracted measures contain errors?

A possible approach combines text measures checked against star ratings with partial-identification methods, allowing uncertainty in those measures to carry through to demand parameters and counterfactual predictions.

AI component
LLM-based measurement
Economic structure
Partial identification
Research target
Demand bounds and counterfactuals

Information & consumer learning

Proposed direction

Learning from selective reviews

How do consumers learn from reviews when owners choose whether and when to share their experiences?

A possible approach combines text-based measures of experience, Bayesian learning, and a model of review disclosure and purchase decisions. This would distinguish changes in owners’ experiences from changes in which experiences become visible.

AI component
Text-based experience measures
Economic structure
Learning and disclosure models
Research target
Information and purchase decisions

Personalization & evaluation

Proposed direction

Policy learning with economic structure

Can a model of consumer demand improve the design of personalized offers under fairness constraints?

A possible approach uses economic structure to inform candidate pricing rules, with their performance evaluated on held-out experimental data. A further direction is adaptive pricing that accounts for both learning and group disparities.

AI component
Policy learning and bandits
Economic structure
Consumer demand models
Research target
Pricing, learning, and group outcomes

An open research agenda

Better measurement. Clearer mechanisms.
More credible decisions.

The common goal is to connect flexible learning methods to economic questions whose assumptions, predictions, and limitations can be made explicit.
