Structural Models × AI · Proposed research

My Research Idea

My research ideas combine economic structure with AI for measurement, estimation, computation, and counterfactual analysis.

Why they fit together

A structural model can keep its assumptions about choice, information, and equilibrium while learning flexible functions from data. AI can operate inside the model: extracting its inputs, estimating its unknown objects, or accelerating its solution. Identification and counterfactual validity still depend on the economic assumptions.

Measurement:

Turn unstructured data
into economic inputs.

Reviews, product descriptions, and images contain attributes that a conventional dataset can miss. NLP and embeddings convert this information into measures that enter a demand or learning model. The economic question is what those measures represent, and how their errors affect the conclusions.

In the literature: Compiani, Morozov, and Seiler incorporate text and image embeddings into mixed logit demand estimation, connecting unstructured product information to substitution patterns.

My research ideaI would like to extend my automobile-review project with Prof. Sha Yang by carrying uncertainty in text measures through to demand estimates and information counterfactuals.

Reading: Compiani, Morozov, and Seiler.

Estimation:

Learn flexible functions
under identifying restrictions.

Machine learning can capture nonlinear first stages or approximate unknown functions in IV models. Instruments, exclusion restrictions, and conditional moments identify the economic object. Regularization, sample splitting, and appropriate inference make flexible estimation useful for economic conclusions.

In the literature: Chen, Chen, and Lewis use ML to learn instruments within a linear IV framework. Chen, Chen, and Tamer study neural-network estimators in nonparametric IV models. Farrell, Liang, and Misra connect deep learning to semiparametric inference.

My research ideaI would like to use flexible estimation in demand models with rich covariates and imperfect measurement, while making the identifying variation explicit.

Readings: Chen, Chen, and Lewis; Chen, Chen, and Tamer; Farrell, Liang, and Misra.

Computation:

Make richer dynamic
models feasible.

Dynamic models become costly as the state space grows. Neural networks can approximate expensive profit or continuation-value calculations. Bellman equations and equilibrium conditions continue to discipline the solution, including in states reached under a new policy.

In the literature: Bodéré’s preschool-market application trains a network to approximate static equilibrium profits, then uses its learned features in value-function approximation. The economic model determines the problem; the network helps make the computation manageable.

My research ideaI would like to explore neural approximations for dynamic consumer learning, and assess their accuracy using optimality conditions and policy-relevant states.

Reading: Bodéré.

Counterfactuals:

Change the rules.
Solve the market again.

A counterfactual specifies what changes and which relationships remain stable. Flexible demand and supply functions can then be used to solve for a new equilibrium and examine prices, choices, and welfare. Credibility depends on identifying variation, data support, and the assumptions carried into the new environment.

In the literature: Chiang, Collison, Magnolfi, and Sullivan combine neural supply estimation with instruments and the Variational Method of Moments. Counterfactual prices must remain consistent with demand and equilibrium. Their combined supply function does not by itself separate marginal costs from markups.

My research ideaI would like to study how flexible supply estimation changes merger, tax, or product-regulation conclusions relative to conventional specifications.

Reading: Chiang, Collison, Magnolfi, and Sullivan.

Selected readings

Papers discussed in my coursework that connect to these ideas. Links point to published articles or author-hosted versions; some working papers have been revised since the lectures.

  1. Demand Estimation with Text and Image Data
    Giovanni Compiani, Ilya Morozov, and Stephan Seiler · 2026 · The RAND Journal of Economics

    Learned representations of unstructured product data as inputs to a demand model.

  2. Mostly Harmless Machine Learning: Learning Optimal Instruments in Linear IV Models
    Jiafeng Chen, Daniel L. Chen, and Greg Lewis · 2021 working-paper version

    Machine learning and sample splitting for instrument construction within a linear structural equation.

  3. Efficient Estimation of Average Derivatives in NPIV Models: Simulation Comparisons of Neural Network Estimators
    Jiafeng Chen, Xiaohong Chen, and Elie Tamer · 2023 · Journal of Econometrics

    Neural-network estimators for nonparametric IV models, with attention to inference and finite-sample performance.

  4. Dynamic Spatial Competition in Early Education: An Equilibrium Analysis of the Preschool Market in Pennsylvania
    Pierre Bodéré · Working paper

    A dynamic competition application connecting economic equilibrium, neural approximation, and policy evaluation.

  5. Market Counterfactuals with Nonparametric Supply: An ML/AI Approach
    Harold D. Chiang, Jack Collison, Lorenzo Magnolfi, and Christopher Sullivan · 2026 working paper

    Flexible supply estimation with instruments, followed by equilibrium counterfactual analysis.

  6. Deep Neural Networks for Estimation and Inference
    Max H. Farrell, Tengyuan Liang, and Sanjog Misra · 2021 · Econometrica

    A framework for using deep-learning estimates in semiparametric inference, with a direct-mail marketing application.
